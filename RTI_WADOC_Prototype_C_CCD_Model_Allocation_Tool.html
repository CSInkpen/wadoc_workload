    What the tool calculates

    For each office, annual workload is the sum of three parts. Recurring case work is active case-months per year multiplied by minutes per case-month for each Washington ONE classification, plus a smaller weight for inactive-status cases. Discrete events (intakes, violations, reports, arrests, release plans, transfers) are annual volumes multiplied by minutes per event, including travel. Organizational work is a fixed amount per office plus an amount per FTE.

    Workload is divided by available work minutes per FTE per year, net of leave, holidays, and mandatory training. Because organizational work per FTE grows with staffing, it is subtracted from available time rather than added as workload. The result is compared with the office coverage floor; the larger number is the staffing need.

    Rules that prevent double counting

    Case-month weights exclude activities modeled as events. Coverage floors are applied as a minimum, never added on top of counted work. Leave and training reduce available time and are not also counted as workload. All parts use one annual basis.

    How case-month weights are built

    Each weight is the sum, across activity families, of occurrences per case-month multiplied by minutes per occurrence. Field contacts add office-specific travel time. Changing a contact standard changes only the frequency; measured durations carry forward. Classifications are treated as separate groups, not a ranked scale, because the Washington ONE assigns them hierarchically from separate offense-specific scales.

    Reentry Centers

    Each center's need is the sum of coverage posts converted to FTE (post hours per year divided by available hours per FTE) and resident-driven case work (resident-months multiplied by minutes per resident-month, plus admissions multiplied by minutes for intake, PREA screening, and release planning), divided by available work minutes. Posts are a floor that does not fall with census; case work rises and falls with residents.

    Positions and cost

    Funded positions come from the budget. Filled positions apply a vacancy rate to funded positions and represent staff actually available. The tool reports both gaps against need and prices them at an annual cost per FTE, so the Legislature can see the cost of funding to need and the Department can see the effect of vacancies separately from funding.

    What is simulated here

    Statewide totals are scaled to public figures: about 13,800 individuals on active supervision (DOC caseload report, August 2026), about 15,000 inactive-status cases (the RFP), and about 626 funded CCO positions (the 2023 workload assessment). The cost reference of $6,072 per active ADP is DOC's standard fiscal-note assumption from its existing supervision workload model, which includes Hearings, Records, and Training. All offices, caseloads, classification mixes, funded positions, and parameter values are invented for demonstration. In the study, caseloads and events would come from OMNI, durations from the time study, frequencies from OMNI and DOC policy, and floors from the design council. Support units (Records, Hearings, CRU, CVL, LRA) would appear as further components with their own demand units.
